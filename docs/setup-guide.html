Getting Geo-Grid running on your computer

1

Install Node.js

3 minutes · one time only

Node.js is a free, safe program maintained by a non-profit foundation. It is the engine this app runs on, in the same way a car needs an engine you never look at. You install it once and then forget it exists.

  1. Open nodejs.org/en/download in a new tab.
  2. Click the large green button labelled LTS. A file downloads.
  3. Open the downloaded file and click Continue or Next until it finishes. Do not change any of the options it offers.
✓
How to know it worked

Nothing new appears on your screen, and there is no new icon anywhere. That is correct. Node.js works quietly in the background and has no window of its own.

2

Download the app

1 minute

The button below downloads the app as a single ZIP file. You do not need to visit GitHub or choose anything from a menu.

Downloads a file of about 1.4 MB.
Nothing is installed and nothing runs yet.

Download the app (ZIP)
On a Mac

The file goes to your Downloads folder and usually unzips itself into a folder. If you only see a ZIP file, double-click it once and the folder appears next to it.

On Windows

Open your Downloads folder, right-click the ZIP file, choose Extract All…, then click Extract in the box that appears.

Drag the resulting folder onto your Desktop so it is easy to find again. The folder has a long name. You can rename it to just geo-grid if you prefer.

✓
How to know it worked

You have a folder on your Desktop. Open it and you will see roughly twenty items, including one called Start Geo-Grid. That is the one you need next.

3

Start the app

2 minutes the first time
On a Mac

In that folder, find Start Geo-Grid.command and double-click it.

On Windows

In that folder, find Start Geo-Grid.bat and double-click it.

!
The first time, your computer will try to stop you. This is expected.

A box says the file “cannot be opened because it is from an unidentified developer”. Click OK to dismiss it. Then right-click on Start Geo-Grid.command (or hold the Control key and click it), choose Open from the menu that appears, and click Open again in the box. You only ever do this once.

A blue box says “Windows protected your PC”. Click the small More info link inside it, then click the Run anyway button that appears. You only ever do this once.

A black window opens with white writing in it. The first time only, it spends about a minute downloading the app's parts. Then your web browser opens by itself and shows the app.

!
Leave the black window open

It is the app's engine. If you close it, the app stops and the browser page stops working. When you have finished for the day, closing that window is how you shut everything down.

✓
How to know it worked

Your browser opens a page headed Geo-Grid Rank Report with a form on the left. If the browser does not open on its own, type localhost:3000 into the address bar yourself.

4

Make your first report

1 minute · free

The form on the left now walks you through four numbered steps. It shows you where you are as you go.

Step 1 of 4 — the business

Fill in the name, the city, and what people search for. Here is a worked example you can copy, or use a real local business you know.

Business name Pacific Coast Assisted Living
City, State or Zip Sunnyvale, CA
Search keyword assisted living sunnyvale

As you type the city, the Grid spacing box picks a size for you and shows a short green note explaining why. Leave it as it is, leave the amber Mock mode box ticked, then click Scan market & find competitors. This part takes a moment, because it searches from 25 different points around the business.

Step 2 of 4 — the competitors

Two cards appear, already filled in. The app picked them from what it found:

  • Market Dominator is the big name locally: the most reviewed of the businesses that turn up almost everywhere on the map.
  • Nearby Direct Peer is a rival round the corner that is still ahead of your client.

Each card shows the review count, the star rating, and one line explaining why it was chosen. If you already know who the client keeps losing work to, click change on either card and type that name instead.

Step 3 of 4 — generate

Click the green Approve Rivals & Generate Executive Audit. This one is quick, because it reuses the search from step 2 rather than doing it all again. You are never charged twice for the same scan.

Step 4 of 4 — the report

The finished page appears on the right, laid out exactly like the picture at the top of this guide. Two buttons sit in the bottom-right corner of your screen:

  • Export / Print PDF opens your normal printer box, already set up to fit one page. Choose Save as PDF instead of a printer to get a file you can attach to an email.
  • Copy Report Email puts the written pitch on your clipboard, ready to paste. Copy Outreach Email gives you the short first email that asks permission to send the report.

The page is built to print on exactly one sheet of US Letter paper, with no web address, date or page number printed on it.

✓
How to know it worked

A headline at the top reads “[the business] captures X% of local searches within 1 mile of your location” (the distance follows the grid spacing), with three maps side by side below it and two short columns: how they rank today, and what moves the pins. Printing gives you exactly one page, with no web address or date printed on it.

!
These first numbers are invented

Mock mode makes up realistic-looking data so you can see the whole thing working without paying anything. The layout and the wording are exactly what a real report looks like, but do not send a mock report to a prospect. Step 5 switches on real data.

To put your own package and price on the bottom strip, open ⚙ Settings and scroll to section 5.

5

Switch on real rankings

10 minutes · this part costs money

To get real Google rankings the app needs an account with a data provider. This is the only part of the whole setup that costs anything. Roughly five cents per report.

  1. Create an account at app.dataforseo.com and add a small amount of credit. Twenty dollars covers around four hundred reports.
  2. In DataForSEO, open the API Access page. It shows your login email and an API password. That API password is a separate thing from the password you use to sign in. Copy both.
  3. Back in the Geo-Grid app, click the ⚙ Settings button in the top-right corner.
  4. In section 1, choose DataForSEO from the dropdown, then paste the login email and the API password into the two boxes that appear.
  5. While you are there, fill in section 4 with your own name and website. That puts your branding on every report you send.
  6. Click Save settings, then click Check setup.
✓
How to know it worked

The Check setup panel shows a green tick beside each line. If anything shows a red cross, the text beside it says in plain English what is wrong. Once the ticks are green, go back to the main page and untick Mock mode. Your reports now use real Google data.

Using it again tomorrow

You never repeat steps 1 and 2. To open the app on any future day, go to the folder on your Desktop and double-click Start Geo-Grid. It starts in a couple of seconds, and the security warning does not come back.

Every report you make is also saved automatically in the output folder inside that same folder, as a PNG, a PDF and a text file. So you can always go back and find one you made last week.

If something goes wrong

Every problem below has a short fix. If you get stuck on any of them, send over a photo of what you are seeing.

The black window flashes and closes straight away

Node.js is almost certainly not installed. Go back to step 1 and install it, then double-click Start Geo-Grid again.

Double-clicking opens the file in a text editor instead of running it

This happens on a Mac when the file loses its permission to run during unzipping. Right-click the file, choose Open With, and pick Terminal.

The report is covered in “Access blocked” writing

That text came from OpenStreetMap's map servers. They are run by volunteers and they block apps that use them for commercial work, sending back a notice instead of a map.

The app now spots this and switches to a different map service on its own. If you still see it, open ⚙ Settings, go to section 3, choose CARTO Positron, and save.

The map mentions an API key

Some map services now want you to create an account. Open ⚙ Settings, go to section 3, and click Test every map service. It tries each one from your computer and shows which actually work, so you can choose one with a green tick.

If none of the free ones work where you are, a Mapbox token is free and takes two minutes. There is a link to it on that same Settings page.

The report shows a plain grey pattern instead of streets

No map service could be reached at all. Check your internet connection, and turn off any VPN you have running. The yellow bar just above the report names every service that was tried and what each one said.

It says “We could not find that location”

The app tries three separate address-lookup services before giving up. Seeing this usually means your internet connection dropped, or a VPN is making those services refuse you. Switch the VPN off and try again.

The guaranteed way past it: open Google Maps, right-click on the business, and click the row of numbers at the top of the menu that appears. That copies its coordinates. Paste them into Advanced → Listing coordinates. The app opens that box for you automatically whenever a lookup fails, so you can paste straight in.

The report found the wrong business

Two things fix this. Either paste the business's coordinates into Advanced → Listing coordinates as described above, or add a free Google Places key in ⚙ Settings section 2, which makes the app match the exact listing rather than guessing from the name.

Anything else

Click ⚙ Settings, then Check setup. It tests each piece of the app one at a time and tells you which one failed and why. Send that screen over and it can be fixed.
